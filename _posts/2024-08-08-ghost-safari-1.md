I have been trying a lot of newbie distilleries recently (<a href="https://duckdrams.com/2024/01/12/the-newbies-part-i/">Newbies Pt I</a>, <a href="https://duckdrams.com/2024/03/15/the-newbies-pt-ii/">II</a>, <a href="https://duckdrams.com/2024/04/20/the-newbies-pt-iii/">III</a>) with the aim of getting through all of them eventually — a task that will take years for the simple fact that new distilleries still keep popping up like malty mushrooms in the ground! Of the 28 distilleries founded in the 2000s that have made their first official release, I think I have now tried all but three (Lagg, Ballindaloch, Arndahoe), and for two of them I already have samples ready.

Then the completionist in me started wondering: How far am I from having tried <em>every</em> Scotch single malt distillery? There are 95 operational pre-2000s-founded distilleries, and I am quite certain I have tried all of them in one form or another.

So the Achievement is beckoning!

But what about closed distilleries? Surely I will never try the original Longrow that closed in 1896. But we can put a threshold at distilleries closing after 1975, because it seems reasonable one could get a sample of those. Ladyburn and Killyloch closed in 1975, before then was Ardgowan in 1952 (now set to reopen), and before then Glengilp in 1937. So then I could say I've tried every distillery producing whisky in the last 70 years!
