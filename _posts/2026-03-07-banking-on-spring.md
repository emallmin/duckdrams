Spring is in the air, and Springbank is in the glass tonight. We start off with a bottle kill of my Springbank 10 (2023 batch), and a chance to compare to a sample from an early 2000s batch. Springbank sticks to its traditional ways of making its signature Scotch, nonethelss, how much variation can we see over the years? Then we top the night off with some flashy "duty paid samples".

## Springbank 10 batch comparison

On the nose, the New has familiar notes of tropical fruits (passion fruit curd), zesty citrus, contrasted with salty sea air, and funky sneakers, rubber. By comparison, the Old shows lots more vanilla, is less tropical and bright, and more evidently peated, with tarred ropes. Sipping, the new is sweet, sour, savoury, and very juicy. Tropical fruits, clementines, along with a farmy, funky peat. The Old has more in the way of peat, less in fruit, being drier, more fusile. For me it's clear that the New is more delicious, since I'm relly into the tropical notes, but others may prefer the stronger pungent punch of the Old. While I highlight the differences here, there is certainly a common DNA. And as the tasting progresses (and tastebuds wear out) they more more similar, but never fully converge. To summarize, the New tastes like more active casks in comparison to the Old, and it has a mellowing of the Campbeltown character that might in part, at aleast, be attributable to the oxidization in bottle.
