



## Old red
N: Fruit-forward, apples, cookie dough, coconut. Something hard-to-define "old school" (or old bottle effect), like a slightly sour grain and sherry note.

P: Really quite sweet, a little thin, but quite pleasantly fruity; orchard fruits, berries, and some vanilla. More of the sherry oak spice in the finish, and actually even a bit soapy.

C: Unoffensive, but also rather uninteresting. The one memorably thing about it is the old bottle note, that I do quite like in this case. 

## Black Ruby
N: Extremely candy-like, with artificial fruit cola and bubblegum, plenty citrus too. Light smoke, wood.

P: Sweet, somewhat candy-like, a bit of smoke. 

C: Not particularly good - it feels rather unnaturally sweet and adulturated, watery. 

## Double black
N: Very weak at first. Then zesty and gingery, turning more rum-y, with hints of peat reek. With time, the peat becomes much stronger, and citrussy.

P: Biscuit-y (digestive), and the peatiest of the bunch by a mile. Otherwise, some vanilla, some coastal dryness. 

C: Can't say much except that it's a quite generic peated dram.

## Green
N: Fersh and fruity, lots of apples, apfel strudel, almond shavings, bit of oak spice, and very subtle coastal smoke. Quite like this nose.

P: Keeping with orchard fruits, especially apples and pears, and cereals in the arrival. Quickly turns more roasted, chocoalty, and smoky-ashy, without much of the vegetal aspects of peat. Golden sultanas and sweetness in the finish.   

C: Not at all bad. The Linkwood is very apparent in the apple note, and I like that. There is some dynamics too. This dram does makes a good case for blended malts in that the peat level can be precsely balanced to add interest and complexity; most single malts tend to be releatively more on/off with that. 

## Gold
N: Rather faint nose, left me stumbling in the dark for long. Then reading about the component distilleries, I could start looking for what's to be expected: A salty, coastal vibe, some goat's cheese with honey, biscuits, golden sultanas, a little liquorice.

P: More expressive than the nose. Salty-sweet, even a little savoury, going on honey, even some waxy honeycomb (though I might be looking too hard), and prominent warming bonfire smoke and some slightly farmy, hay-ish notes. Also quite spicy, peppery, grassy, leaving a bit of bitterness.   

C: The nose is a let down. I'm having to really tease some life out of it. The palate is better, though there's something about the sweetness and lightness that's undersells the experience.

## Ultimate
Sawdust and rice cookies, old damp casks, hessian. It's really unusual in this way, even compared to other drams I'd call dunnage-y. Then suddenly I'm hit with a distinct keffir lime. ... And then coming back to the nose a little later, the dunnage is mostly gone - was it just an illusion?

P: Definitely some mini-bottle defect (cardboard note), but not totally wrecked. Rather sweet and savoury; cola, nuts, leather, and smoke.

C: I have to grant it, that it's bringing up memories of being on distillery warehouse tour to life more than almost any whisky! Not in the intensity or appeal of the notes, but something in the charater. But is it entirely the whisky itself, or could it be the mini-bottle defect?
