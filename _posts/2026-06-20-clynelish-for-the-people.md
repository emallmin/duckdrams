For the last few years IB Clynelishes were few and far between, appearing every once in a while as a cat-themed secret Highland or Sutherland, and often with a heavy price premium. Now, economic desperation or magnanimous generosity (you be the judge) has had Diageo releasing a lot of Clynelish casks for the IBs and the market is flooding with that briney-sweet, disputedly waxy, nectar.

My overall conclusion from this cavalcade of Clynelishes:

### Common profile
- **Nose:** Citrus, specifically grapefruit, is the main constant on the nose for me. Often spongecake/vanilla, as well as chalky/mineral/petrichor, sometimes goes in a floral/summer meadow direction
- **Palate:** always a lovely texture, briney and oily and sweetish, fruitiness of varying intensity
- **Finish:** gooseberry, often slightly metallic
- **Waxiness** varies substantially between bottlings; often you find just a little if you actively search for it, more rarely is it clear and prominent
Overall, it's a lovely profile and actually very hard to go wrong, at least if you stick to gentle-moderate cask influences

### Bottle comparison
- They are indeed relatively similar in profile. They are all good, but the prices varied from €45 to €100+ for similar specs.
- Some have more uniqueness/character. I would highlight the Adelphi (very prominent passion fruit note) and the TB 12 (sour-metallic-fatty in a good way) in that regard.
- The TB 8yo is the clear winner in price/performance and captures the profile very nicely. Hard to justify spending double on the Adelphi, even though I really enjoyed that one. For a CS I would say JE or DT were the best price/performance.
- The DT is maybe the waxiest one (but possibly confounded by the PC cask and it being my most recent pour)
- As discussed in another thread the SV 100 proof is comparatively a let down (a bit hot and plain). I wouldn't bash it as harshly as others have, because it's not bad as such, but it's hard to recommend given the alternatives
