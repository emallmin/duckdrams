Craigellachie is probably the only distillery for which I can say that the official releases (their prime-numbered 13, 17, 19, 23 years olds) have consistently outperformed all the independent bottlings I've tried, about 10 or so. It's therefore at some risk of disappointment that I have ordered a bottle of Whisky Agent's 5th anniversary bottling of Craigellachie. On paper though, everything is spot on: 23 years old, ex-bourbon barrel, and 45.7% ABV that I presume is a natural cask strength - a huge green flag for me, as I've consistently found this to imply a concentrated flavour experience without excessive booziness. Curiously this is only on of 60 (!) bottles, but it appears this cask was a share. Responsible for the independent bottling operation on WA's behalf is Caskhound, that I have positive prior experience with. Then there's the price: with a current anniversary discount, €138, very attractive indeed. Despite all the positive signs, to guard myself (and to still my immediate curiosity) I also bought a 4cl sample of the very same liquid. If it's unconvincing, I could throw the bottle into auction and not be at much of a loss. Why not buy the sample first, and then order the bottle? Reasonable, but that would involve two shipping costs, whereas now I qualified for free shipping. But more importantly, with only 60 bottles produced I expected them to sell out almost immediately. Somehow that has not yet happened six days later, and here I am, sample in hand.

Is it finally the indie that beats the official releases? Hard to say without a direct comparison, but the price is at least €80 below the official 23, and the bourbon cask profile is certainly more to my taste than the official 17yo. So in the end, I'm very pleased that I got a bottle, and I look forward to truly get to know it some time in the future.
