Lot 34
Nose:
Very little alcohol on the nose, the aromas are dense but extraordinarily vibrant. Lots of fruits, candied orange peel, dried apricots, golden apple juice, and tropical "tutti frutti". Old leather couches, precious wood furniture - it is neither stuffy or overoaked, but there is an earthy dampness here. Meshing with that is a complex herbal and spiced mixture: saffron, to be sure (which clicked with me after reading another set of tasting notes), cinnamon, earl gray tea and liquorice with a tad of bitterness. In all, very hamonious and complex, rewarding time in the glass and 

Sip
The naturally reduced alcohol lands well, and the palate is mouth-covering. So lively! Perfectly ripe mangoes are at the fore, and fragrant honey, ginger ale, then turns minty, and leaves more of the spices (saffron, liquorice, ) in the relatively long finish.   

Comments
Amazing how 80 years in a cask has not reduced this cognac to a tannic sludge, but it has all the vigour of youth and wisdom of age. The best cognac I have had. Probably the best cognac I will ever have.



41
Nose: 
Incredibly rich and <em>thick</em>. Strongly of toasted hazelnuts, apricot marmalade, strawberry coulis, and a fair bit of oak, and some earthy, mineral notes peeking through. Delicious.
Palate:
Intense, juicy with all manner of berries and nuts, nougat, baking spices, heading into eukalyptus, mint, and liquorice. Rather drying and tannic, but not to bad effect.

Comment:
It is exceptionally good, and would be the winner in many noble spirit comparisons, but it is not as charming and complex as the Rue 34.
