# Tobermory mos23012
2026-10-17
N: Light and fresh nose of peach and cream, other orchard fruits like apples and pear, perhaps a dash of white pepper, along with hints of dunnage and minerality. A nice, honest malt, but rather simple. 
P: Malty-sweet, a touch saline. Vanilla and spongecake, sweet biscuits, and toasted oak, gooseberries, a little sweet grapefruit. Very distillate driven, and it's a robust spirit. Tastes like a higher ABV but still very drinkable.
Before: I'd have to guess it's a young whisky, 5-8yo, so probably from a new distillery. How about GlenWyvis or Dalmunach. Probably 54% but drinks more like 49%. Ex-bourbon cask, a refill.
After: That's funny. So ABV and cask I fookin' nailed. But it's a venarable 15 years of age from the venerable Tobermory. Post-hoc I can discern a bit more of the age, but it's still a rather straightforward, albeit clenly executed dram, owing to a rather inactive cask, surely. 
S: 84

# Tutti frutti
2026-10-17
N: Rather shy but also elegant, with a mix of tropical fruit, oak spice 
P: Sweet and a really quite salty. Here, the sherry strikes me as way overcooked. There's a kind of fatty Oloroso/PX thickness here that becomes insipid. Raisins, cocoa butter, orange milk chocolate,roasted and salted peanuts, caramelized sugar, some autumnal leafy, earthy notes.
before: What could it be? I guess a PX finish, 50+ ABV but not super-strong, probably a young distillery and 8yo, but could be up to 14yo and a traditional one as well. Has the kind of mouthfeel I get when I tried to add sherry directly to a dram and overdid it; weirdly sweey and fatty.
after: Aha, it's not sherry but indeed some wine finish that definitely too strong for my taste.
S: 80

# Experiment
Now here spontaneously comes a little experiment. That Tobermory was very spirit-driven but a bit aneamic, wheras that Tutti-frutti was to winey. But maybe the combination of the two works? Let's try a 50/50 ratio. The nose is an improvement over both. Copleixty of minerality, wine, fattiness. The palate is much better than the Tutti-Frutti alone, but that wine is still too overbearing.

# Adelphilaphi
N: There's peat here, round and earthy, dried herbs, musty cellar or animal farm, some malt grist. And a fruiter side heavy on dried mangoes, and with some oak aged white wine. It's a well integrated nose. 
P: Umami-bomb, with saltiness and sweetness too. The ripe mangoes and peaches are fully entagled with earthy peat, with lashes of iodine, tart grapefruit, seawater. Finished on hot popcorn, golden sultanas,     
C1: This has to be Islay. Does Ardnahoe have anything tasting this mature yet? Several Islay's are in the running, but I'll have to put my money on Laphraoig. (Would be humiliating if it's highland peat!) Drinks so easy, and no booziness on the nose, so I at first thought this might be 46%, but conclude that's an illusion and we're somewhere around 50%. Not obivous sherry notes, but there's more to it than a bourbon cask, so I will guess refill sherry, or perhaps a mix of bourbon and sherry casks. Trying it next to the Thompson Bros Bowmore 19 I recently review, it's clearly way less complex and mature, but perhaps more satisfying in it's elemental yet coherent character. Could be 8-15 years; I'll guess around 10.
C2; Yes, it's Laphroaig! Refill bourbon, 56.5%, 14 years. Not miles away from my guess. I did well. I have absolutely zero memory of having bought this sample, though, so it is still unexpected. Sadly, being an Adelphi Laphroaig means the price for a bottle will be obscene. Maybe that's why I bought a sample?
S: 88

## Fettercairn 16
2026-10-25
N: Fresh fruity, with apples, clementines, turning then towards candy. There's a summery, minerally, and dusty (perhaps dunnage-y) side as well. Breathing hard into the glass, hints of waxiness.
P: Juicy, full of ripe fruits steering in the tropical direction, banana, with some wine gums as well. Somewhat spirity, moderate alcohol burn
CB: This is delicious whisky in a style that I like, though nothing remarkable. Could be many things, from Speyside to Ireland. I tend to think it's going to be more than 46% but less than 50%, with that higher strength not making so much sense since I think the age will be around 10, but could also be 15. Ex-bourbon most likely, but perhaps even a white dessert / fortified wine. The kind of bottle that would sit comfortably at the €55-65 price point.
CA: Huh, in hindsight I remember ordering this sample long ago. I think it's delivers pretty well what one can expect. Maybe a little too spirity for the age.
S: 85

## SS? Glasgow 2016/2023 - 7- Virgin oak
2026-10-28
N: Brown sugar, lots of oak, slightly sour grape, digestive biscuits, rustic bread, cacao, noori sheets, a phenolic aspect pointing to a very light peat.
P: It has this interesting taste of feeling sweet while actually being quite dry. Savoury, somewhat briny, and some oak tannins. Again with digestive biscuits, taosted bread, sea water, pickles and liver paté, and a deeply earthy and warming peat, quite strong but not at all overwhelming. There's some acidity cutting through, like apple cider vinegar.   
C: I'm going to guess for a peated version from a new non-Islay distillery, either of Torabhaig, Raasay, or Lochlea. It feels young, but with a satisfying maltiness that I associate with local barley and unhurried whisky-making. It feels above 46% but not a high cask strength, so I'm going to guess an unorthodox reduced strength of, say, 48.4%, that some of the new distilleries sometimes adopt. The casks taste like a mix of bourbon, sherry, and even wine casks. Good quality here, plenty of personality. A successful crafting of a small batch release, in my opinon. 
C2: And it's a Glasgow. Funny thing is, as I was peeling off the paper covering the label, I thought. What will I write if it's a Glasgow? I'll write, "Right idea, wrong distillery". Right idea, wrong distillery. First-fill bourbon with virgin oak finish is a little surprising, but I can see how that's giving some of the oaky and rustic qualities. Shamefully drinkable at 56%! 
S: 86

## Starward 10356
D: 2026-11-01
N: A noble nose of old wood, leather, raisins and prunes, rather vinous, glögg (mulled wine), hints of marzipan
P: That is unsually acidic, and a bit boozy, tannic and dry, cutting through an otherwise dense texture. Sweetness comes at the side of the tongue in a little while. Raisins, milk chocolate, orange, spices like clove, vanilla.       
C: On the nose I could see it being an old Glenfarclas. But with the palate I'm thinking it's not necessarily so old, maybe 14, but the cask is doing a lot of (good) work. The mouthfeel points to red wine or port, though I could see it being sherry. Speyside or Highland. Probably one that's drinkable despite high ABV, around 56%. Perhaps some Signatory 100 proof, so 57.1%?Amazingly, no MBE descpite the sample coming from whic.de. 
C2: Ah, I should stick to my guns, it is indeed red wine! And amazingly only 4 years old. There sure was something unusual about it, funny that it from being Australian. Different maturation timescale due to climate.
S: 87

## Tamdhu 301558
D: 2026-11-01
N: Tropical fruit, light, phenolic peat; almost a bit farty/rotten, but within the range of fascinating, not offputting. Passion fruit, apricot, and then I can't decide between mouldy celler, pebble beach, or wet woodland?
P: Tropical fruits like pinapple, coconut water, apricot jam, sugar cane juice, but not very sweet; a bit woody, and a cold, ashy smoke rises.   
C: Certainly tasty whisky, not a style I haven't seen before, but one which is not the most common. It's some form of non-Islay peated. Could certainly be Loch Lomond. Not so strong, 46% or even lower. Ex-bourbon or some kind of refill.
C: Now that's surprising. Tamdhu 12, refill oloroso. I'm thinking that cask was either a bit funky or most likely it held some peated spirit before. Or I've somehow deluded myself.
S: 84
