


## 34 Invergordon

N: Fragrant oak, strawberry jam, and toffee. To the woodsy aspect add rather dusty notes, like old paper, cedar, and, yes, some wood glue too, but not sharp. On the confection side: vanilla and cinnamon bun dough (buttered and cinnamon'd).

P: Silky, buttery, pleasantly sweet. That unbaked cinnamon bun description is so apt. Strawberries cooked to a pale mush. Some leather. Overall totally cosistent with the nose. Warming spices in the finish, maybe even some cayenne.

C: The dessert-y profile is characteristically old grain, but it is delicious. Splendid value for money

S: 87


## Glen Garioch

N: Very distinct yet hard to describe. Rather austere, mineral, even industrial, while also throwing tropical fruits and desserts at you. The industrial notes are hovering around the slightly burnt, ashy, rubbery (though not a clear sulphur cask). The tropical fruits I'm getting are in the territory of papaya, star furit, squirt of lime. The confectionary side is buttery, creamy, fatty, in a sherry fasion. That latter effects grows with time, and it becomes honied, waxy and also develops a floral, herbal aspect; green pepper, lavender... A nose one could explore for a long time! A while in it has completely transformed. 
P: It's salty, briny, muscular, not immediately sweet but grows in time. Leather, musk, oak, dried fruits, and quite minty. A smoky quality, like some nuts that are overtoasting. Very long finish, with waxy residues. The 2cl are not quite enough.  
C: Very complex and interesting, and not easy to wrap one's head around at first. Definitely brings to mind some well-aged drams of closed distilleries selling at ridiculous prices. Yet this one has not sold out in years at sub-€200. Whiskybase score at a meagre 86.35 with a statistically respectable vote count of 53. Opinions range from "I don't like it" to "gem among the gems". Definitely not a beginner's malt. In my opinion, one to keep an eye out for in the auctions!

## Ben Nevis

N: Big and bolsterous nose. Roasted coffee beans, ginger ale / cola, leather, rancio, musty cellar, cigar room. And bursting through all of that: perfectly ripe pineapple, pommegranate, oranges... With time, cheesier, fattier. Lots of spices to pick out too: clove, anise... let's just say chinese five spice.
P: An explosion of tutti-frutti and coffee-chocolate in every sip. Evolving into old-cask notes of dried fruits, leather, and tobacco leaf: the alcohol, oak tannins and spice near enough simulate the prickliness of the nicotine. (I once steeped a tobacco leaf in whisky for some months, let me tell you.)   
C: This is among the best whiskies I have ever tasted. Not top 5, but surely top 15. Not a marvel of elegance, but man does is hit the right spot!

S: 92

## Bowmore

N: Not a peat bomb. The peat is there, but equal in presence to a coastal minerality, and underripe tropical fruits. Also a sort of flour note, some coffee
P: Thick and juicy, unctious, chewy, and saline. Big fruits, sweet canteloupe melon, salted ham, the peat hits in the early finish before the flavours revert to sweet and fruity, but more candy-like, aromas of foam banana, and as well custard cream. 
C: I like the nose more than the palate. Don't get me wrong, the palate is a treat, and very flavourful (I love the balance of tropical fruits and peat), but there's somethign slightly stale or flat to it, however contradictory that may sound.
S: 88
