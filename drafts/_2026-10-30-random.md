
I saw this sample in an online shop, and I realize I have a bottle of this, and it could be fun to know what it's like without opening my bottle.

## Aultmore
N: Fresh, crisp, lots of pears, apples, melon, honey and white wine, both with a floral quality. Some waxiness with time and water.
P: A malty and fruity sweetness, quite delicious, with honey, melon, grapes, a little sweet grapefruit. That generous fruitiness is met with a robust and more austere spirit character coming through. Neat, it borders on boozy; with water transforms to peppery (like virgin ollive oil), dry biscuity, and even mildly tobacco-y. Sadly some MBE in the finish, especially with water. (That means the rest of sample goes into making whisky sauce)  
C: It's a style I like, and align with my expectations for an Aultmore. I feel it deserves more oxidation and water tinkering, and the absence of MBE, to come into its own. If I didn't already have a bottle, I would not rush to buy one based on this first impression. Having one, I feel it might find its moment in good time, but also is not one of the unique bottles that definitely could not go to auction, if push came to shove.
S: 84

## Glencadam
N: A very distinct nose, not at all what one generically expects from 14 years in a first-fill bourbon cask. There's toffee, sour cherries, lots of fruity coffee, orange peel, a bit rubbery.
P: Salty-sweet, caramel, old leather, tropical fruits, subtle (cigar) smoke. It's really a kind of profile I would expect only from much older bourbon cask maturation, and have often mistaken for sherry in blind tasting. 
C: This is cask magic. Best I can get it for presently is €100. At €70 I would have just bought a bottle without much hesitation. Now I really have to think. Thinking in terms of a whisky tasting organizer, I would love to include this as an example of what a bourbon cask is capable of.
S: 88

## Croftengea
N: So recognizably Croftengea! That south-east asian vibe of aromatics like keffir lime and thai basil, smoked fish and perhaps some crayfish, and lots of pungent tropical fruits. Also some vanilla, and a helping of allspice. Certainly phenolic, slightly vomity — but in a good way.
P: Very concentrated tutti-frutti fruit juice, again that vomity (sounds too terrible!) and sour dish-rag quality of a gueze, and a phenolic, mischevious peat.
C: It was so fun to immediately get thrown back to Croftangeas of my early whisky days. 
S: 87
